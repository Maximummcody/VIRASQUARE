# ViraSquare Brand and Trust Foundation

## Proposed brand promise

**ViraSquare helps small businesses turn real products and real customer needs into content they can confidently use.**

## Short description

ViraSquare is a thoughtful daily content partner for small businesses. It helps owners prepare product flyers, captions, buyer replies, educational posts, and a realistic weekly content rhythm from their own saved business and product details.

## Design boundaries

The final ViraSquare identity should use an original vector-first mark, a navy and electric-blue core palette, and simple reproducible geometry. It must avoid generative-art texture, gradients used as decoration, generic AI-symbol styling, stock motifs, and ambiguous imagery. The logo should remain clear in a browser tab, mobile app icon, social profile, and Meta settings.

## Assets to finalise after an owner selects a direction

| Asset | Primary use |
|---|---|
| Horizontal wordmark | Website header, legal pages, Meta app listing, email signature |
| Standalone V mark | App icon, favicon, social profile image, avatar |
| One-colour variants | Light and dark backgrounds, watermark and document use |
| Social profile image | Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, and support channels |
| Small icon set | 16, 32, 180, 192, and 512 pixels |

## Trust assets before Group 4

Before public Instagram/Facebook publishing, ViraSquare should have a public Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, data-deletion request page, support contact, final app name and logo, and a custom domain. The final Group 4 Meta submission should also include the selected use case, a secure redirect URL, a data-deletion request URL, and a clear recording of the owner connection and publishing journey.

## Verified Meta readiness references

Meta's current Instagram app setup documentation says that a business verified connection is required before publishing an app, and its business-login setup calls for redirect, deauthorization, and data-deletion request URLs. Meta's Instagram Platform overview says that serving Instagram professional accounts that the app owner does not manage requires Advanced Access, App Review, and Business Verification. The content publishing documentation also requires publishing media to be publicly accessible at the time Meta fetches it.
