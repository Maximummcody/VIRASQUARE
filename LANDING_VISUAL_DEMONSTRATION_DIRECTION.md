# Landing Visual Demonstration Direction

## Recommendation

**Yes, ViraSquare should add visual proof in the two areas you highlighted.** The current sections have the right message and structure, but they leave too much empty space where visitors could be feeling the product result. This is an opportunity to show the work in a more memorable way.

It will only become messy if every section gets a large visual. The better move is to make **two moments** more visual and leave the trust section and footer calm. That contrast will make the page feel more intentional and more premium.

## Visual 1: Show the product-post transformation

Replace the blank image placeholder inside **What you save** with a polished example product canvas. It should use a neutral fictional product, such as a clean everyday watch, bottle, shoe, or fashion item, and it should be labelled **Example product**. It will not use a real customer’s product or a generated result claimed to be from a real business.

The canvas should visually move through this simple story:

| Moment | What the visitor sees | What it communicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Saved product** | A crisp product frame with its saved name, price, and key detail lines. | ViraSquare starts with information the owner confirms. |
| **Content becomes ready** | A composed post preview slides or fades into view beside it. | The product can become a finished visual, not just a vague suggestion. |
| **Selling package** | The current caption, buyer-reply, and next-angle stack stays visible. | The result supports the whole selling conversation. |

The animation should be a one-time, short left-to-right transformation while scrolling. It should never look like a loud carousel or continually change the product. This is the best place to make the section feel powerful because it demonstrates ViraSquare’s clearest value in seconds.

## Visual 2: Put real format previews inside the three content cards

The three large cards should use their lower open space for compact visual previews. Each preview should look like a small, refined content example, not a blank decoration.

| Card | Small visual preview | Design approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product post** | A miniature social flyer composition with one product silhouette, clear price zone, and brand strip. | Navy frame, Electric Blue detail, and a small **Example** tag. |
| **Educational content** | A 2–3 mini-card carousel stack with a clear headline block, supporting copy lines, and one simple content-relevant graphic cue. | Light background, Sky Blue shapes, no dense fake copy. |
| **Trust building** | A compact “What customers should know” advisory-style post with one proof/check icon and short supportive lines. | Warm white surface with Navy typography and a restrained Blue signal detail. |

Each preview will be built with ViraSquare’s own visual language, rather than generic stock graphics. The three previews should feel different enough to demonstrate range, while keeping the same overall quality and visual confidence.

## What I would not add

I would not add a stock-person photo, a generic robot/AI illustration, random lifestyle imagery, fake customer testimonials, fake screenshots, made-up sales numbers, “trusted by” logos, or constant movement. Those choices would make the site look more like marketing decoration than a real business tool.

I would also not use a real user’s flyer, product image, business name, or customer message on the public page unless that person explicitly gives permission. The demonstrations should stay clearly fictional and visibly labelled as examples.

## Design balance after the change

| Page area | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | Keep the existing example workspace. It already shows the daily-content direction well. |
| Product post section | Add the strongest visual demonstration: product in, complete post and selling package out. |
| Three content-type cards | Add small content-format previews to use the open lower space and prove variety. |
| Owner-control section | Keep calm and mostly text-led. It is a confidence and trust moment, not a product-gallery moment. |
| Final invitation and footer | Keep as they are. Their job is clarity and a final deliberate action. |

## Expected impact

This would make the landing page feel more like a product visitors can already imagine using. It reinforces the strongest ViraSquare difference: the owner moves from real saved business information to content and selling support that is organised, reviewable, and useful.

The public page would still remain honest: the visual outputs are examples of format and flow, not proof of customer outcomes or real user activity.
